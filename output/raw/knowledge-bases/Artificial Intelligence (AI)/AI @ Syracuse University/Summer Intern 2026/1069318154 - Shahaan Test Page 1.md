Pumice is the only rock that floats in water. It forms when frothy, gas-rich lava cools so fast that the bubbles get trapped inside, leaving the rock more air than mineral. After the 1883 Krakatoa eruption, enormous rafts of pumice drifted across the Pacific for years, ferrying barnacles, crabs, and even living plants to new shores.
